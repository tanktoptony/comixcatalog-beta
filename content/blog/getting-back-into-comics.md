I fell off comics for a long time. Not on purpose. Life happened, the long boxes went into a closet, and one day I realized I had not read a new issue in something like ten years.

Getting back in was weirdly hard. That surprised me. I knew these characters. I grew up on the 90s X-Men cartoon and Batman: The Animated Series, I had the cards, I had the opinions. But when I actually tried to pick up a current book, I hit a wall of twenty years of continuity, four different Spider-Man titles, three reboots I had missed, and a forum full of people who all disagreed about where to start.

So I did the low-tech thing. I walked into Graham Crackers Comics here in Chicago and asked the guy at the counter where to start.

He said: House of M.

I read it that weekend. I loved it. I was back.

That conversation is honestly half the reason ComixCatalog exists. Not everybody has a counter to walk up to, or a guy behind it who gives a good answer. So here is the answer I wish I had been able to look up.

## First, the thing nobody tells you

You do not need to catch up. There is no catching up. Marvel and DC have each published something like sixty years of interconnected stories and nobody alive has read all of it, including the people writing it. Every "where do I start" guide that begins with a 40-book reading order is written by someone who forgot what it felt like to be new.

You need one good book. Then another one. That is the whole method.

## Pick a character you already care about

The character is the hook. If you loved Wolverine as a kid, start with Wolverine. If it was Batman, start with Batman. Do not start with whatever is critically acclaimed this month if you have no feelings about it. Feelings are what get you through the confusing parts.

## Then pick one of these

These are entry points that work. They stand on their own, they are easy to find (in print, digital, or in a bin at your shop), and they are good.

**If you liked the X-Men cartoon:** House of M, obviously. Then *X-Men: Second Coming* or, if you want to go all the way back, the Claremont and Byrne run starting around Uncanny X-Men #108. You will understand why everyone your age is still talking about the Dark Phoenix Saga.

**If it was Batman: The Animated Series:** *Batman: The Long Halloween*. It is a year-long mystery, it is drawn beautifully, and it is the book the Nolan movies were quietly borrowing from. After that, *Batman: Year One* and the Batman Adventures series, which is literally the cartoon in comic form.

**If you want Spider-Man:** Ultimate Spider-Man #1. It restarts Peter Parker from the beginning, in 2000, with no continuity to know. It runs 133 issues and you can read it as a kid or as an adult and get a different book each time.

**If you want something that is not superheroes at all:** *Saga*. Or *The Walking Dead* from #1, which is black and white, was expected to get cancelled around issue 6, and instead ran 193 issues and changed television.

**If you liked Avengers movies:** *Avengers: Disassembled* into House of M into *Civil War*. That is a straight line through the years when Marvel was rebuilding itself into the thing you saw on screen.

## Read collected editions first, not single issues

This is the part that trips people up. Single issues are what collectors chase. Trades (the paperback collections of five or six issues) are what readers should buy. They are cheaper per page, they do not fall apart, and you can read a whole story in one sitting instead of waiting a month and forgetting who everyone is.

Read trades until you find the series you cannot put down. Then go find the single issues. That is when collecting starts, and that is the part this site is for.

## When you start collecting, write it down

Everyone who has ever gotten back into comics has bought the same issue twice. I have done it. You will do it. The fix is boring: keep a list.

ComixCatalog is the list. Every series, every issue, every printing, with the covers linked so you can tell at a glance whether you already own the one in your hand. You add the books you have, you add the books you want, and the wantlist becomes the thing you carry into the shop instead of a vague feeling that you "need some Daredevil."

It is free. Create an account, search for the first series you fell for, and start clicking the issues you own. That is the whole onboarding.

## And go to the counter anyway

Every good shop has a person who has read everything and will tell you what to read next if you ask. Ask. Buy something from them while you are there. The counter is not obsolete. The counter is the best recommendation engine ever built. This site just makes sure you remember what they told you.

If you are in Chicago, mine is Graham Crackers. Tell them a guy on the internet sent you.
