I built this thing and I still forget features exist, so here is the tour I would give a friend. Five minutes, five screenshots, no jargon.

## 1. Search for a series, not an issue

Type the series name in the big search box. "Amazing Spider-Man", "Batman", "Saga", whatever you collect. You get the series first, grouped by run, with the years next to each one so you can tell the 1963 run from the 2018 one at a glance.

![Search results for Amazing Spider-Man, showing each volume with its years](https://wlrcxnzdywzxgrcqvctc.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/comic-covers/blog/how-to-use-comixcatalog/02-search-results.jpg)

That grouping is the whole point. Spider-Man has been restarted more times than my router. The site knows which "#1" is which so you do not have to.

## 2. The series page is the run

Click a series and you get the whole run, every issue with its cover, in order. This is the page I spend the most time on. Scroll it like a long box.

![The Amazing Spider-Man series page with the full run of covers](https://wlrcxnzdywzxgrcqvctc.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/comic-covers/blog/how-to-use-comixcatalog/03-series-page.jpg)

Once you are signed in, this page also shows your progress on the run: how many you own, how many you are missing, and which ones. That number is either very satisfying or very motivating depending on the day.

## 3. The issue page is where the details live

Click any cover and you get the issue itself: publisher, date, the variants and printings if there are any, and the buttons that matter.

![An issue page showing the cover, details, and the save and wantlist buttons](https://wlrcxnzdywzxgrcqvctc.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/comic-covers/blog/how-to-use-comixcatalog/04-issue-page.jpg)

**Save to your library** means you own it. **Add to wantlist** means you are hunting it. If you own it, you can also set the grade, the grading company and cert number if it is slabbed, what you paid, and a note ("bought at C2E2, guy swore it was a 9.8"). All of that is optional. I have plenty of books logged as "I own this" and nothing else.

## 4. Your library and your wantlist

The library is everything you have marked as owned, sorted however you want, with the values next to the books that have recent sales data. You can export it as a CSV, or as a PDF with the covers on it, which is the thing you want to have already made when someone asks what your collection is worth.

The wantlist is the other half, and honestly the better half. It is the list you carry into the shop. It is the reason you stop buying the same issue twice.

## 5. Your profile is public, if you want it to be

Every collector gets a page at comixcatalog.com/u/yourname. It shows your collection, your wantlist, and your stats. You can turn any of that off in settings, but people mostly leave it on, because a comics collection is a thing you want to show somebody.

![A public collector profile showing their collection grouped by series](https://wlrcxnzdywzxgrcqvctc.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/comic-covers/blog/how-to-use-comixcatalog/05-public-profile.jpg)

That is the tour. Search, series, issue, library, profile. Everything else on the site is a detail on one of those five things.
