Collecting comics is a social hobby that mostly happens alone in a room with a long box. You buy a book, you bag it, you board it, and the only person who hears about it is whoever happens to be standing nearby. Usually that person did not ask.

This past week was about fixing that, and about fixing the number at the top of your collection while I was at it.

## Your collection's value, from a real price guide

Until now the value on your books came from what people were asking for them on eBay. Asking prices are optimistic. Everybody's copy is a 9.8 in their heart.

Values come from PriceCharting's price guide now. Their numbers are built from sold listings, and they're matched to each of your copies at its grade. A slabbed 9.4 gets the 9.4 price. A raw copy gets the raw price, and if you've recorded its condition, it never comes out worth more than a slab at that grade would be. Books the guide doesn't list still fall back to eBay asking prices, and those stay labeled as asking.

You'll see the guide in a few places:

- the total on your shelf and in your library, and the value chart over time
- each issue page, raw and graded, with a link to the book's page on PriceCharting
- your wantlist on your shelf, so you know what that missing issue should cost before you go hunting
- marketplace listings, next to the asking price, so you can tell a deal from a wish

The PDF and CSV exports cover your whole collection now too, and the PDF runs in series order. If your insurance agent has to read it, at least it'll be in order.

## Who else has it

Every issue page shows the collectors who have the book, want it or are selling it. Pick one and message them about that exact book. The message carries the book with it, so nobody has to ask "which one?"

That's the part I'm happiest about, because it turns "I wonder if anyone has this" into a conversation.

Messages have no send caps anymore, and you can see when yours was delivered and read. Yes, that means they know you read it.

## Trade matches

In your library you can mark any copy Open to trade. The [Matches page](https://www.comixcatalog.com/matches) then looks for collectors who have something on your wantlist and want something you have. Both directions, matched by issue. When it was switched on, it found matches for nine members on the first day.

## A feed, reviews, lists and comments

Your shelf has a Collector feed now. Following shows what the people you follow added, wanted, listed and finished. Everyone shows the whole site. It is a very good way to find out somebody owns a book you didn't know existed.

You can rate any issue from one to five stars and write a review. Variants and newsstand copies share their issue's reviews, so the ten covers of one book don't split the conversation ten ways.

You can make [lists](https://www.comixcatalog.com/lists): a reading order, the best covers of a run, the books that got you into comics, whatever you want. Each one gets a link you can share.

And you can comment under an issue, a list or a review. Replies and @mentions show up in your inbox.

One note on privacy: if your profile is private, your books stay out of all of this. You can still use every bit of it yourself.

## Picking the right version

In your library, you can switch a copy to the newsstand edition, a later printing or a variant cover by tapping its cover or scanning its barcode. On an issue page, picking a variant cover switches the whole page to that edition: price, barcode, credits and guide value. Newsstand collectors, this one's for you. I know you've been patient.

One small thing: your badges page now shows how many collectors have each badge.

## Imports that read shorthand

Bringing a spreadsheet over from another app used to stop at every title it couldn't place as written. "ASM 300" meant nothing to it.

Now the [importer](https://www.comixcatalog.com/switch) takes a second look at those rows. It works out that "ASM 300" is The Amazing Spider-Man #300 from 1988, and that "X-Men Vol. 2 #30" is the 1991 series. Every guess has to be a real book in the catalog, and nothing gets added until you tick it. Only the title, issue, publisher and year are read for this, never your grades, prices or notes.

## Email you control

In [Account settings](https://www.comixcatalog.com/account), you choose how you hear about replies, @mentions and comments: right away, in a summary at most once a week, or not at all.

There's also a weekly digest: what the collectors you follow added, wantlist books someone listed, your trade matches and your collection's value. It only goes out when there's something new, and every email has a one-click unsubscribe.
