## Short002WhoWroteThis · ~40 s · ON CAMERA + covers

**Hook:** I collected these comics for years before I noticed who wrote them.

> I collected these comics for years before I noticed who wrote them.
>
> East of West. The Manhattan Projects. I bought them for the covers. Look at these. One color, one image, almost nothing else.
>
> And the art inside Manhattan Projects is completely nuts. Famous scientists drawn like Garbage Pail Kids.
>
> Then I finally read the credits. Jonathan Hickman. The guy who wrote Secret Wars. The guy who wrote House of X.
>
> He'd been in my long box the whole time. So check yours. You might own more Hickman than you think.

**Visuals:** Tony holding his own copies (CAM) → East of West #1 and Manhattan Projects #1, full frame → interior face crop (MISSING, see ASSETS.md; fallback MP #20) → Secret Wars #1 and House of X #1 flash on "the guy who wrote" → back to Tony.
**Ending:** "You might own more Hickman than you think." Hard cut to black.

## Short002ManhattanProjects · ~35 s · VO over covers

**Hook:** What if the atomic bomb was the boring part?

> What if the atomic bomb was the boring part?
>
> That's the pitch for The Manhattan Projects, by Jonathan Hickman and Nick Pitarra. World War Two. Los Alamos. Oppenheimer, Einstein, Fermi, Feynman.
>
> Except the bomb is just the cover story. The real science going on underneath is way, way weirder.
>
> There's a talking Soviet space dog. There's an AI built out of Franklin Roosevelt. And issue one has a twist about Oppenheimer that I'm not going to ruin.
>
> Twenty-five issues from Image. Start with Volume One. It's called Science Bad.

**Visuals:** MP #3 ("The Bomb") on the hook → MP #1 → one cover per scientist, cut on each name → MP #6 on "space dog" → MP #1 focus on the face → Science Bad TPB to end.
**Ending:** the trade title as the punchline. Hold the TPB cover 1.5 s.

## Short002EastOfWest · ~35 s · VO over covers

**Hook:** The Civil War never ended. A comet ended it.

> The Civil War never ended. A comet ended it.
>
> That's the setup for East of West, by Jonathan Hickman and Nick Dragotta. In this America, the war drags on until 1908, when a comet hits Kansas. The treaty splits the continent into seven nations.
>
> Jump to 2064. The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse are real. Except Death has gone rogue, and he's got a very personal reason.
>
> Forty-five issues, and it actually has an ending. Start with Volume One, The Promise.

**Visuals:** Text slam "1865?" crossed out, then "1908" → EoW #1 → timeline graphic, comet → "2064" → EoW #2, #4, #6 → EoW #1 focus on Death → EoW #45 → TPB.
**Ending:** "it actually has an ending" is the payoff. TPB cover last.
