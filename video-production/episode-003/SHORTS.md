## Short003DiscogsForComics · ~45 s · VO over the long boxes

**Hook:** Discogs started as one guy cataloging his own records.

> Discogs started as one guy cataloging his own records. Electronic music. His favorite. That's it.
>
> Then other collectors showed up and added what they owned, and it turned into the database for basically every record out there.
>
> I'm building something like that for comics. It's called ComixCatalog. And when a friend handed me two long boxes of nineties comics, I found out I'd started the same way he did. Narrow.
>
> The Marvel and DC stuff went in easy. The weird stuff? Some of it wasn't there. Because I'd built it around what I knew.
>
> A real catalog has to handle what real collectors actually own. Not just what I like. That's the job.

**Visuals:** Overhead hands opening the two long boxes, then a Discogs screen recording of a release page, then hands flipping through the boxes, then SCREEN: a mainstream run filling in, then SCREEN: a search with no results only if still true on recording day, then hands place the last book into a sorted pile.
**Ending:** "That's the job." Cut.

## Short003MilkAndCheese · ~35 s · VO over covers, Tony's hands holding the book

**Hook:** The second issue of this comic is called Other Number One.

> The second issue of this comic is called Other Number One. The third one? Third Number One.
>
> This is Milk and Cheese, by Evan Dorkin. Slave Labor Graphics, nineties. A carton of milk and a wedge of cheese who hate everything and deal with it through violence. The tagline is "dairy products gone bad."
>
> I found Milk and Cheese in a long box a friend gave me. And when I went to add it to the comic site I'm building, it wasn't there.
>
> That one's on me. It's on the list.

**Visuals:** Milk & Cheese #2 cover, focus on "other number one" → #3, focus on "3rd number one" → #1 → HOST: Tony's hands with the physical copy from Corey's box → SCREEN: empty search → black.
**Ending:** "It's on the list."
**Recording-day rule:** if Milk & Cheese is on ComixCatalog by then, change the last two lines to: "It wasn't there. It is now. That's the whole point."

## Short003ScudVendingMachine · ~40 s · VO over covers

**Hook:** In this comic, you buy your assassin from a vending machine.

> In this comic, you buy your assassin from a vending machine.
>
> It kills its target, then it blows itself up. That's the deal.
>
> Then one of them, Scud, figures that out. So he doesn't finish the job. He puts his target in the hospital and takes freelance work to pay the medical bills. Because as long as the target's alive, so is he.
>
> Scud the Disposable Assassin, by Rob Schrab. It started in 1994, stalled at issue twenty on a cliffhanger, and he came back and finished it in 2008.
>
> Fourteen years to finish a comic about a robot who refuses to finish the job. Honestly? Perfect.

**Visuals:** Scud #1 (or Tony's physical copy) → #2 → #3 → #20 on "cliffhanger" → Image #21 (2008) → hold.
**Ending:** "Honestly? Perfect."
**Note:** The Scud #1 image we have is a second printing. If Corey's copy is a first print, film that instead.
