## 1. The six stats (the card back)

**OK, primary source.** Marvel.com's Victor von Doom character page carries Marvel's Power Grid as data: Intelligence 6, Strength 4, Speed 5, Durability 6, Energy Projection 6, Fighting Skills 4. Same six numbers as the brief's provisional grid, so they can be called "Marvel's official power grid" on screen, credited "Source: Marvel.com".

Same page, for the card or a fun aside: height 6'2" (6'7" in armor), weight 226 lbs (415 lbs in armor). Marvel lists his powers as "Magic, Superhuman Strength, Genius Intelligence".

**Sorcery is not on Marvel's grid.** Keep it as our own badge, labeled "CC EDITORIAL". Proposed rubric is in section 6; the card shows UNRATED until Tony picks a number.

**Trading card history (for the "why stat bars" line):**
- OK: The 1991 Impel *Marvel Universe Series II* cards had a "Power Ratings" bar graph on the back, 1 to 7, for Strength, Speed, Agility, Stamina, Durability and Intelligence (Wikipedia; Trader Cracks' set guide). Doom is card #88 in that set.
- VERIFY: whether *Series I* (1990, Doom is #60) already had the bars. Sources disagree, so say "the early-'90s Marvel Universe cards" rather than naming the year.
- Marvel's current six-category grid (with Energy Projection and Fighting Skills) is a different system from those card bars. Don't call our card "the 1991 ratings".
- [TONY]: did he have these cards as a kid? He was 7 or 8 in 1991, so if he did, that's the cold open.

**Why the bars are imperfect (narration material, no claims needed):** a 1 to 7 bar can't show preparation, can't show borrowed power, and Doom's whole history is preparation and borrowed power. That's what the three contexts are for:
- **A · Standard Doom**: the armor and the man. The card.
- **B · Doom with a plan**: robot doubles, a rig built for one target, a machine built out of a henchman. Still Doom's own power, multiplied by prep time.
- **C · Borrowed cosmic power**: the Silver Surfer's, Galactus's, the Beyonder's, the Beyonders'. Temporary every time. Glowing overlay only.

## 3. The armor and the toolkit (context A and B)

All OK from Marvel.com's bio unless marked:
- Titanium armor; personal force field generator and other shielding; concussive blasts from the gauntlets; flight from a miniaturized propulsion system.
- He keeps upgrading it himself; Marvel says he "continued to augment and accessorize" it.
- Doombots: autonomous robot duplicates that have fooled heroes "for long periods of time". Marvel's word, safe to quote.
- Time platform; devices to cross into other dimensions.
- One of the most complete libraries of spells and artifacts on Earth; Marvel says his sorcery "rivals or even exceeds his scientific knowledge". That quote is the bridge into the sorcery chapter.
- Marvel says he refuses to accept Tony Stark as a genius. Good for the "Iron Man comparison" line.
- Context B examples, all in the comics: the rig that drains the Silver Surfer (FF #57), the machine he builds out of Klaw's body to drain Galactus (*Secret Wars* 1984 #9 to #10), the scan of the Beyonder's energy his armor takes in *Secret Wars* #1 and turns into a weapon in #10.

## 5. Context C: borrowed cosmic power

### Fantastic Four #57 to #60 (Dec 1966 to Mar 1967), Lee, Kirby, Joe Sinnott inks
OK, and FIX the ending, because the real ending is the best Reed vs. Doom beat in the episode:
- **#57 "Enter Doctor Doom"**: Doom invites the Silver Surfer to his castle and gives him a tour. The Surfer starts to notice how he treats his servants. In the lab Doom distracts him with a view of the cosmos he misses, hooks up the gear, and drains the Power Cosmic. Then takes the board.
- **#58 "The Dismal Dregs of Defeat"**: Doom flattens the FF. He slows the Thing's molecules until he's a statue. The Torch's explosion does nothing. Reed tells the team to stand down. Doom lets them live, because surviving it is the humiliation.
- **#59 "Doomsday!"**: Doom flies around the world showing off. Reed builds a device and tests it on Ben, and says the key is making Doom angry.
- **#60 "The Peril and the Power!"**: Mid-fight, Reed's "anti-cosmic" flying wing hits Doom and flies off into the sky. Doom chases it, straight into the barrier Galactus put around Earth to keep the Surfer in. Flash. Power gone, the board goes back to the Surfer.
- So: Reed didn't out-power him. He out-annoyed him. That's evidence for the ego line, not just opinion.
- V0.1 "shows the limits of trying to hold cosmic power": FIX to the barrier version above.
