## 03-the-man-in-the-mask.wav · The man in the mask

Doctor Doom first shows up in Fantastic Four 5. July 1962. Stan Lee and Jack Kirby.

And his first move is a big one. He shows up at the Baxter Building, holds Sue Storm hostage, and sends Reed, Ben and Johnny back in time to steal Blackbeard's treasure for him.

Ben ends up captain of a pirate ship. The crew calls him Blackbeard. And Reed brings Doom back a chest full of chains instead of the gems.

That's his first issue. Time travel, pirates, and a hostage. He did not ease in.

> SHOW: FF #5 cover, caption "Fantastic Four #5 · July 1962 · Lee / Kirby". [SCAN: Ben as "Blackbeard", FF #5.]

His real name is Victor von Doom. He's born in the small kingdom of Latveria, to Romani parents, and he loses both of them young.

His mother dealt in magic. She made a bargain with a demon, and it cost her her soul. Her magic is where Victor starts learning. Remember the mom. She comes back.

> SHOW: Books of Doom #1 cover. [SCAN optional: young Victor with his mother's things, Books of Doom #1.]

He's brilliant. He gets a scholarship to a university in New York, where one of his classmates is Reed Richards.

That goes about how you'd think.

Victor builds a machine to reach other dimensions. It blows up in his face. He gets expelled.

So he goes to Tibet, where monks forge him his first suit of armor. And the mask. Then he goes home, overthrows the king, and renames the capital Doomstadt.

Which tells you a lot about the guy. He could have named it anything.

> SHOW: FF Annual #2 (1964) cover, caption "The Fantastic Origin of Doctor Doom!". Books of Doom #6 on "overthrows the king". "Doomstadt" over the FF #84 cover.

The face. The comics have told that one a few different ways. Marvel's own bio says the explosion only left a few small scars. Other versions blame the mask itself. Either way, it's one of the best designs in comics. You know who's walking into the room before he says a word.

If you want the whole origin in one place, read Books of Doom. Six issues from 2006, Ed Brubaker writing, Pablo Raimondi on art. It's told like an interview, with Doom telling his own story. Which is exactly how Doom would want it told.

> SHOW: Books of Doom #1 to #6 grid, then the 2025 trade cover. Mask close-up on the last line.

## 04-the-armor.wav · A: the armor and the numbers

Now the Doom on the card. Standard armor. No stolen cosmic power. Just Victor on a normal day.

Marvel's profile lays out what the armor does. It's titanium. It has a personal force field. The gauntlets fire concussive blasts. And it flies.

And he never stops working on it. Marvel says he "continued to augment and accessorize" it. Which is the fanciest way I've ever heard anybody describe a weapons upgrade.

> SHOW: Marvel.com profile capture, armor paragraph highlighted. Card back, EQUIPMENT list lights up.

So here are the numbers. Marvel's power grid, out of seven.

Intelligence, six. Strength, four. Speed, five. Durability, six. Energy projection, six. Fighting skills, four.

> SHOW: Segment bug "CARD BACK" (the recurring stats segment). Card back, each meter fills as it's named. Caption "Marvel.com Power Grid · standard armor".

Look at what's high. Brains, toughness, and firepower. Now look at what's in the middle. Strength and fighting. He's not a brawler. He doesn't need to be. If Doom's in a fistfight, something already went wrong.

Fun one from the same page. Doom is six foot two. In the armor, he's six foot seven and four hundred fifteen pounds.

> SHOW: Card back, vitals row highlighted.

Now, the obvious comparison is Iron Man. Genius, builds his own armor, flies around blasting people. Doom even took the name Iron Man for a while, in Infamous Iron Man back in 2016.

And Marvel's profile says Doom refuses to accept Tony Stark as a genius.

Different Tony. I assume he feels the same way about me.

> SHOW: Infamous Iron Man #1 (2016) cover. Marvel.com profile line highlighted.

But here's what the Iron Man comparison misses. Tony Stark doesn't do magic.

## 05-sorcery.wav · Sorcery

Marvel's profile says Doom's sorcery "rivals or even exceeds his scientific knowledge."

Think about that. This is one of the best scientists in the Marvel Universe, and Marvel's saying the magic might be the bigger half.

> SHOW: The quote over the Triumph and Torment cover, credited Marvel.com.

The book that shows it best is Doctor Strange and Doctor Doom: Triumph and Torment. A graphic novel from 1989. Roger Stern writing, Mike Mignola on art. Yes, that Mike Mignola. A few years before Hellboy.

I hadn't read this one before we started on this episode. It's exactly the kind of book I want this series to dig up.

It opens with a contest. Sorcerers from all over, competing for the title of Sorcerer Supreme.

Doom shows up, and he gets one rule. Turn off the tech in your gauntlets. Magic only.

He does it. And it comes down to two people. Doctor Strange and Doctor Doom.

Strange keeps the title. Doom comes in second. With the armor switched off.

> SHOW: [SCAN: the contest, gauntlets off, Triumph and Torment.] Fallback: the cover, slow push.

Now here's the twist. In this contest, the winner owes the runner-up a favor. So Strange has to grant Doom one request. And he's nervous. He figures Doom's going to ask for help taking over the world.

Doom tells him he doesn't need help with that.

What he wants is his mother. Her soul's been trapped with Mephisto for years, and he wants Strange's help getting her out.

So the two of them go into Mephisto's realm together. And Doom gets her out.

That's the story where Doom gets to be the hero. Sort of. He's still Doom.

> SHOW: [SCAN: Mephisto's realm, Triumph and Torment.] Fallback: Mignola cover crop.

So, the sorcery badge. Marvel doesn't rate magic, so we built our own scale, one to seven. A five means you're a master. You can go head to head for the Sorcerer Supreme title. A six means you hold the title, or beat whoever does.

Standard Doom gets a five. He went toe to toe with Strange, magic only, and came in second. That's a five.

> SHOW: Sorcery medallion pops out of the card back, value 5, label CC EDITORIAL. Rubric strip under it (text card 2 of 8).

And the six? He's had that too.

Blood Hunt, 2024. The world's gone dark, and Strange needs Doom's help to fix it. Doom says to cast the spell, he needs the title of Sorcerer Supreme. Strange hands it over, on one condition. Give it back when the world's saved. Doom gives his word.

Doom saves the world. Then he tells Strange he's only getting started, and announces himself Sorcerer Supreme to the entire planet.

To be fair, he did save the world. He just didn't give it back.

That leads right into One World Under Doom, in 2025, where he declares himself ruler of the whole planet. But that's not standard Doom. That goes in column C.

> SHOW: Blood Hunt #4 and #5 covers. One World Under Doom #1 cover. Card overlay flickers to 6, then back to 5.

## 06-with-a-plan.wav · B: Doom with a plan

That's A. Now B. Doom with a plan.

Same armor. Same guy. The difference is time. Give Doom a few weeks and a reason, and the numbers on that card stop meaning much.

Start with the Doombots. Robot copies of Doom. They're in his very first issue. The Fantastic Four fight a robot double of him in Fantastic Four 5. And Marvel says they've fooled heroes "for long periods of time."

So any time Doom loses, you kind of have to ask. Was that even him?

> SHOW: [SCAN: the robot double, FF #5.] Fallback: FF #5 cover crop on Doom.

Fantastic Four 10. 1963. Doom walks into Stan Lee and Jack Kirby's studio. Stan and Jack are right there, drawn as themselves. He has them call Reed over. Then he gasses Reed and swaps minds with him.

So the two guys who created Doom end up working for him. In their own comic.

> SHOW: FF #10 cover. [SCAN: Doom in the Lee and Kirby studio, FF #10.]

Fantastic Four 84 through 87. 1969. S.H.I.E.L.D. sends the team into Latveria. And they end up in this perfect little village. Nice people. Clean streets.

It's a prison. The whole picture-perfect village is Doom's prison.

> SHOW: FF #84 to #87 grid, push on #84.

And then the big one. Emperor Doom. Marvel Graphic Novel 27, from 1987. David Michelinie and Bob Hall.

Doom takes the Purple Man's mind control, boosts it with his own tech, and points it at the whole planet. And it works. Doom actually rules the Earth. Everybody goes along with it, because they've been told to.

The one hero it doesn't work on is Wonder Man.

That's B. No cosmic power. Just Doom, a plan, and a machine.

> SHOW: Marvel Graphic Novel #27 cover. Card back with B lit on the context strip.

## 07-power-cosmic.wav · C: the Power Cosmic

And then there's C. Borrowed power. Doom's been doing this since 1966.

Fantastic Four 57. Doom invites the Silver Surfer to his castle and gives him the tour. And the Surfer starts noticing how Doom treats the people who work for him. Not great.

Then they get to the lab. Doom shows him a view of the cosmos, the thing the Surfer misses most. And while he's looking, Doom hooks him up and drains the Power Cosmic right out of him.

Takes the board, too.

> SHOW: FF #57 cover push. [SCAN: the drain, FF #57.] Card: POWER COSMIC overlay ramps in.

Issue 58. Doom goes after the Fantastic Four, and it's not close. He slows down the Thing's molecules until Ben's basically a statue. Johnny hits him with an explosion, and it does nothing. And Reed tells his own team to stand down.

Doom lets them live. My read? Leaving Reed alive to think about it is the real win.

> SHOW: FF #58 cover (Doom on the board).

Issue 59. Doom flies around the world showing off. Meanwhile, Reed's building something. He tests it on Ben. And he figures out the key isn't more power. It's making Doom angry.

Issue 60. In the middle of the fight, Reed sends a little flying wing at Doom. It smacks him and takes off into the sky. And Doom, being Doom, chases it.

Straight up. Right into the barrier Galactus put around the Earth to keep the Surfer from leaving.

Flash. The Power Cosmic's gone, and the board goes back to the Surfer.

Reed didn't out-punch him. He out-annoyed him.

> SHOW: FF #59, then FF #60 covers. Overlay snaps off on "Flash." Card back drops to baseline.

And that's why the cosmic stuff doesn't go on the card. He had it for four issues. Then he lost it because Reed made him mad.

## 08-secret-wars-1984.wav · C: Secret Wars, 1984

Eighteen years later, he does it again. Bigger.

Marvel Super Heroes Secret Wars. 1984. Jim Shooter writing, Mike Zeck on pencils. A cosmic being called the Beyonder grabs a bunch of heroes and villains, drops them on a planet, and tells them to fight. Whoever wins gets whatever they want.

Everybody remembers that Doom stole the Beyonder's power. What people forget is how. He doesn't go straight at the Beyonder. He goes through Galactus first.

Issue 1. Doom follows Galactus toward the Beyonder and gets knocked back. But his armor scans the Beyonder's energy on the way. He keeps that.

> SHOW: Secret Wars (1984) #1 cover. BEYONDER overlay hint, then off.

Issue 9. Doom breaks out, grabs a villain named Klaw, and builds a machine. Using parts of Klaw. Like, his body.

Issue 10. He uses that machine to drain Galactus's power. Then he goes after the Beyonder. And he starts losing. Badly. The Beyonder is literally taking him apart.

So Doom reaches for a weapon he built into his chestplate, using that scan from issue 1.

And he takes the Beyonder's power.

> SHOW: Secret Wars #9, #10 covers. [SCAN: the Klaw machine, #9 or #10; the chestplate weapon, #10.] BEYONDER overlay ramps in.

Issue 11. God Doom. He builds a tower two hundred miles high. He offers the heroes a wish. They say no. And he tells Captain America his next plan is to free his mother's soul.

There's the mom again.

Here's the catch, though. He can't sleep. If he lets his guard down, the power goes wild.

Issue 12. Klaw keeps feeding his doubts. The power gets away from him. And the Beyonder, who never really left, takes it all back.

> SHOW: Secret Wars #11, #12 covers. Overlay retracts.

That's twice. Twice he got the power. Twice he couldn't keep it.

## 09-god-emperor.wav · C: God Emperor Doom

The third time, he kept it a lot longer.

Secret Wars. 2015. Jonathan Hickman writing, Esad Ribić on art. Nine issues. This is the one people mean when they say God Emperor Doom.

Short version. The multiverse is ending. Whole realities are getting wiped out, and the beings doing it are called the Beyonders. Doom, Doctor Strange and the Molecule Man turn it around on them. And when it's over, the Molecule Man hands the Beyonders' power to Doom.

> SHOW: Secret Wars (2015) #1 cover. GOD EMPEROR overlay ramps in.

So Doom takes what's left of reality and stitches it into one world. Battleworld. Every piece is run by a baron who answers to him. The police force is made of Thors. Doctor Strange is his sheriff.

And Doom is God. Not a god. God.

He took more than the world, too. Sue Storm is his wife. Valeria and Franklin, Reed's kids, are his kids now.

He didn't just beat Reed. He moved into Reed's life.

> SHOW: Secret Wars (2015) #2 to #8 grid. [SCAN optional: the Battleworld map, #1 or #2.]

In issue 4, he kills Doctor Strange. His own sheriff.

Then Reed shows up. Reed survived the end of everything too, and he is not thrilled about the new family arrangement.

Issue 9. Reed and Doom finally face off. And the Molecule Man, who's the real power source here, strips most of the power off Doom so it's a fair fight. Just the two of them.

Reed says if he'd had that power, he would have done better with it.

And Doom admits it.

That's when the Molecule Man gives the power to Reed. And Reed uses it to start rebuilding the multiverse.

> SHOW: Secret Wars (2015) #9 cover. [SCAN: the admission, #9. This is the one scan worth chasing.] Overlay retracts on "admits it."

Last page. Back in Latveria, Doom takes off the mask. His face is healed, and he's laughing. Marvel's bio says Reed healed his face.

Reed beats him, then fixes his face. Honestly, that might be the meanest thing Reed's ever done to him.

## 10-reed.wav · What actually beats Doom

So what actually beats Doctor Doom?

Look at the three times he had cosmic power. In 1966, Reed makes him mad, and he chases Reed's gadget into a wall. In 1984, his own doubts take him down. In 2015, he admits Reed would have done better.

Nobody out-punched him. Every time, he lost it in his own head. And two out of three times, Reed Richards is the one in there with him.

> SHOW: Reed vs. Doom split frame: FF #60 / Secret Wars (1984) #12 / Secret Wars (2015) #9.

Here's my take. And it's just my take. Doom's fine with other people being powerful. Galactus, the Beyonder, whoever. He'll just take it. What he can't handle is somebody knowing better than he does. And there's exactly one guy who keeps doing that.

He's also got options most villains don't. If the science doesn't work, there's magic. If the magic doesn't work, there's a robot double. And if all of that fails, he still runs a country. He'll get you next year.

That's why one power number is never going to capture him. And honestly, that's why he's fun.

Doctor Doom is not Iron Man with a cape. Here's the proof.

In 1989, Marvel put out Triumph and Torment. Doctor Strange and Doctor Doom. Roger Stern and Mike Mignola.

What he wants is his mother's soul back from Mephisto.

So yeah, he builds the armor. He also nearly won the Sorcerer Supreme title with the armor switched off.

And in 2024, he finally got the title. He just never gave it back.

## short-3-god-emperor.wav · "Why God Emperor Doom doesn't count"

When people say Doctor Doom beat the Beyonder, they're right. It still doesn't count.

Here's Marvel's own power grid for Doom. Out of seven. Intelligence six. Strength four. Speed five. Durability six. Energy six. Fighting four. That's Doom in his armor.

Now, 1984. The original Secret Wars. Doom builds a machine out of a villain named Klaw. Literally out of him. Uses it to drain Galactus. Then takes the Beyonder's power. He's a god for about two issues, until his own doubts let the Beyonder take it back.

2015. Jonathan Hickman's Secret Wars. Doom gets the power of the Beyonders and builds what's left of reality into Battleworld. God Emperor Doom. And at the end, he admits Reed would have done better with it.

Borrowed power. Temporary, every time. So on our card it's an overlay. Not the baseline.

Standard Doom is scary enough. Full Power Profile on the channel.
