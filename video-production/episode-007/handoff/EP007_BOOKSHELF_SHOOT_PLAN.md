# COMIXCATALOG EPISODE 007 | TONY'S BOOKSHELF SHOOT SHEET

**Goal:** Film the precise pieces where viewers should SEE Tony talking in front of his bookshelves, with real Batman books visible over his shoulder. This is a hybrid episode. Most history and panels remain voiceover. **You are not filming an 18-minute talking-head video.**

**Companion:** `EP007_CLAUDE_VOICE_EDIT_HANDOFF.md` contains the authoritative full text. These on-camera blocks match its CAM A labels, verbatim.

## Set and camera

- Stand or sit naturally in front of your shelves, framed **mid-chest to a little above your head**. Keep the camera at eye level. Leave negative space to one side for occasional cover overlays.
- Put **Batman books on the shelf behind one shoulder**. Real readable spines or covers are more convincing than a staged display, but avoid a chaotic background. If available, feature Absolute Batman, Azrael/Knightfall, Court of Owls, and your new Dark Knight Returns copy. Only use what you actually own.
- Keep the same chair/standing position, camera distance, lighting, mic and clothing for all ten takes. Use a tripod, horizontal **4K 16:9 if available**, 30 fps or 24 fps consistently. Don't switch orientation for the main video.
- Use soft frontal light and a dimmer warm shelf lamp if available. Avoid a bright window behind you. Turn off buzzing HVAC/fans when possible.
- Record 5 seconds of room tone. Clap once for sync if audio is separate. Check a 15-second test for echo and clipping. Look at the lens rather than the screen.
- For each take, **two seconds of quiet before and after**. If you stumble, pause and restart the sentence. Don't stop recording for every stumble.
- Give Claude/your editor the raw camera files with camera audio, even if a cleaner mic recording is captured separately.
- Shoot **a second, tighter 3/4 crop** of the two most personal beats (CAM A 03 with the book and CAM A 05 Azrael) only if time allows. The 4K master can usually be cropped.

## Primary shot order: film everything in one session

### CAM A 01 | Tamu: the opening hook
**Placement:** 01 cold open. **Framing:** Medium close, direct to lens. **Prop:** None.  
**Intent:** You're telling someone a real work story, not reciting a TED talk.

> I was working this pretty lousy bartending job recently, and one of the guys there, Tamu, turned out to be a huge Batman fan.
>
> He's about twenty years younger than me, and we discovered we were both reading *Absolute Batman*.
>
> Now, I've been a Batman fan since I was a kid in the early 90s. So as we got talking, I started filling him in on some of the older Batman lore.

**Edit out:** After “lore,” transition to Absolute Batman characters and supporting panels.

### CAM A 02 | Azrael reveal
**Placement:** 01 cold open. **Framing:** Same camera angle. **Prop:** Optional Azrael book visible in your hand, or point naturally to an off-screen comic graphic in post. Do not need to hold up a cover you don't own.

> Which naturally brings me back to the time Batman got his back broken and was replaced by this guy.
>
> And I drew this guy constantly when I was a kid.

**Edit:** Cut to Batman #500 artwork on “this guy.” If you have no physical Azrael item, deliver the lines straight to lens and let the edit do the reveal.

### CAM A 03 | The unread Dark Knight Returns copy
**Placement:** Section 02. **Framing:** Slightly wider so the book fits. **Prop:** Your actual newly purchased copy of *The Dark Knight Returns*. **Must-have.**

> Now here's something I should probably admit.
>
> Despite collecting comics since the early 90s, I'd actually never read *The Dark Knight Returns*.
>
> I know. Somehow.
>
> I've known the story, I've seen the artwork, and I've been aware of its influence for years. But I only finally picked up my own copy recently.
>
> So I'm not going to sit here and give you some definitive review of a book I haven't read yet.

**Action:** Show the front cover gently at “picked up my own copy.” Do **not** pretend to read or finish it. Capture a separate 10-second clean insert of the front/back cover after speaking.

### CAM A 04 | Batman Returns Christmas memory
**Placement:** Section 03. **Framing:** Medium close. **Prop:** None required. If you've actually obtained the Michael Singer book, hold it; otherwise we'll use a researched cover in editing.

> I remember getting a *Batman Returns* movie book for Christmas. I'm pretty sure it was Michael Singer's official movie book from 1992.
>
> It had all this behind-the-scenes material, photographs, costumes, production designs.
>
> I spent a lot of time looking through it.

**Edit:** Dissolve into authentic book art and film imagery. No need to overplay architecture, clowns, or Catwoman here. The later VO does that briefly.

### CAM A 05 | Azrael and childhood drawings
**Placement:** Section 04. **Framing:** Medium close, most animated take. **Prop:** A real Azrael comic if owned; any surviving drawings only if genuinely available.

> Azrael.
>
> Man, I loved this guy.
>
> I drew him constantly when I was a kid. His original costume, the armored Batman suit, all of it.
>
> If you remember what superhero comics looked like in the mid-90s, you probably understand why.
>
> Everything had armor and blades and pouches and weird accessories, and some of these guys looked like getting dressed in the morning required a team of engineers.
>
> Azrael fit right in.

**Edit:** Let the first two lines breathe. Cut to costume art on “His original costume.” If you find drawings, get a close-up scan/photo separately; never recreate them and call them yours.

### CAM A 06 | Court of Owls personal anchor
**Placement:** Section 05. **Framing:** Same bookshelf angle. **Priority:** Helpful but optional if running short.

> I was reading this when it came out.
>
> And their first major storyline, *The Court of Owls*, is still one of my favorite ideas for a Batman villain.
>
> Because it's not just one guy in a costume trying to take over Gotham.
>
> It's a whole secret society.

**Edit:** The last line takes us into Court of Owls panels and the labyrinth visual.

### CAM A 07 | Absolute Batman enthusiasm
**Placement:** Section 06. **Framing:** Medium close. **Prop:** Absolute Batman issue or collected book if you own one, but not required.

> So let's talk about *Absolute Batman*.
>
> Scott Snyder comes back, this time with Nick Dragotta on art, and they give us this enormous, working-class Batman.
>
> No inherited billions. No Wayne Manor. No Alfred raising Bruce from childhood.
>
> And Batman is built like somebody fed a refrigerator steroids.
>
> I love it.
>
> I love the design, I love Dragotta's artwork, and I especially love how Snyder takes all these established pieces of Batman lore and rearranges them.
>
> Because when you've been a fan for a long time, half the fun is recognizing what he's doing.

**Edit:** Use a full-screen Absolute cover for the punchline, then compare traditional and reinterpreted villains.

### CAM A 08 | Tamu callback
**Placement:** Section 06 after lore comparisons. **Framing:** Return to exact opening framing. **Intent:** Generous and a little self-deprecating, not schooling Tamu.

> And this is where Tamu comes back into the story.
>
> We were both already reading *Absolute Batman* when we realized we had that in common.
>
> But as we were talking, I started explaining some of the older connections.
>
> Who these characters traditionally become, what their relationships with Bruce usually look like, and some of the history behind Bane.
>
> And some of it was news to him.
>
> Which I thought was really cool.
>
> Not because he needed me to explain the comic to him. He was already enjoying it.
>
> It was more that I had decades of completely useless Batman trivia rattling around in my head, and suddenly somebody actually wanted to hear it.
>
> It's a rare and beautiful thing.

**Edit:** Hold your face on the last two lines instead of cutting to a stock bar scene. Do not invent photos or footage of Tamu.

### CAM A 09 | The Tony Stark joke
**Placement:** Section 07. **Framing:** Medium close. **Intent:** Dry, almost throwaway.

> But at the center of it, Batman's still a guy with no superpowers.
>
> He's a brilliant detective with a ridiculous amount of money, incredible technology, and probably a better conscience than Tony Stark.
>
> Which isn't necessarily setting the bar all that high.

**Edit:** A tiny beat after the joke. No comic reaction meme needed.

### CAM A 10 | Final invitation and sign-off
**Placement:** Section 07 ending. **Framing:** Same as opening. **Prop:** Batman books in background. **Intent:** Warm, not salesy.

> And now I want to know which version of Batman got you hooked.
>
> Was it the animated series? Michael Keaton? Frank Miller? The New 52? Or are you just getting into him with *Absolute Batman*?
>
> And please tell me somebody else spent the 90s drawing that armored Azrael suit.
>
> If you liked this video, subscribe. I've got plenty more comics I want to talk about.
>
> I'm Tony, this is ComixCatalog.
>
> Thanks for watching.

**Edit:** End with ComixCatalog logo/title card, not more talking head.
