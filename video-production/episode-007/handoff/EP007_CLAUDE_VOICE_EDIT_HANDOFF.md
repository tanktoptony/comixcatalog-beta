## Video spine

Tony's childhood Batman was the Burton movies, animated series, games, and the astonishing armored Azrael designs, not an encyclopedic reading of every arc. He later read *Court of Owls* on release and now loves how *Absolute Batman* reworks old lore. At a bartending job, he and a much younger coworker Tamu independently discovered they were both reading *Absolute Batman*. Tony could add context about Batman's older villains and relationships. The episode is about how each generation finds a Batman, while the old material makes the new versions richer.

## 07-why-batman-works.wav · Closing

> SHOW: Batman Returns book cover, BTAS, Azrael, Court of Owls, Absolute Batman.

[VO]

I think part of what makes Batman so enduring is that he's just incredibly easy to get into.

Little kids love Batman.

You've got the costume, the cape, the Batmobile, the gadgets.

He's cool before you even know anything about him.

And then you start getting into the comics and realize there's all this history underneath.

Dozens of writers and artists, different versions of Gotham, different Robins, different interpretations of the villains.

You could spend years reading Batman comics and barely scratch the surface.

[CAM A 09 | BATMAN VS TONY STARK LINE]

But at the center of it, Batman's still a guy with no superpowers.

He's a brilliant detective with a ridiculous amount of money, incredible technology, and probably a better conscience than Tony Stark.

Which isn't necessarily setting the bar all that high.

[END CAM A]

[VO]

And there's something appealing about that.

He's not Superman. He can't fly. He doesn't have laser eyes.

He's a guy who gets up every night, puts on a costume, and tries to do something about the problems in his city.

Granted, most of us don't have a billion dollars to throw at our problems.

Or a Batmobile.

But still.

What I find interesting is how many different versions of that idea I've enjoyed.

As a kid, I was drawing Azrael's armor without really understanding the whole story behind it.

In 2011, I was reading *Court of Owls* as it came out, watching Snyder and Capullo build something new out of Gotham's history.

And now I'm reading *Absolute Batman*, enjoying all the ways Snyder is messing around with characters I've known since I was a kid.

And talking about those characters with somebody twenty years younger than me.

That's pretty cool.

You don't have to have grown up with the same Batman to enjoy talking about him.

And you don't need decades of continuity knowledge to appreciate a new story.

But sometimes knowing that history adds something.

And sometimes you discover that a character you thought you knew has a lot more to him than you realized.

Which is why I finally bought *The Dark Knight Returns*.

Forty years late, but I'll get there.
