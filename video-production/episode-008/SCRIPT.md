### 02-the-recommendation
**VO**

A few years ago, I picked up a comic called *The Nice House on the Lake*.

It was recommended to me by Challengers, my local comic shop here in Chicago. The shop was right across the street from the bar where I used to work, so it was a pretty convenient place to spend my money. Apparently, working in a bar wasn't enough. I needed another expensive hobby directly across the street.

I don't read a ton of horror comics. I've read *The Walking Dead*, and I've enjoyed some other survival-oriented books, but horror isn't necessarily what sends me wandering into a comic shop.

I do, however, love a good mystery.

And that's what grabbed me about this book.

I love stories where you're trying to figure things out alongside the characters. Where everybody has a piece of the puzzle, everybody's behaving a little strangely, and you start questioning what you know about each person.

It's one of the reasons I enjoy the *Knives Out* movies so much.

*The Nice House on the Lake* scratches that same itch. It just happens to be considerably more disturbing.

### 03-the-invitation
**VO**

The setup is simple enough.

There's a guy named Walter. He's a little strange, but seemingly harmless. And he invites a group of his friends to spend a week at this absolutely beautiful lake house.

You've got ten guests, mostly people in their thirties, all coming together for what sounds like a pretty fantastic vacation.

And Walter has apparently arranged everything.

The house is gorgeous. The setting is gorgeous. You can basically have whatever you want.

There's just one small complication.

The world outside ends.

And now everybody's stuck there.

You can't leave the house. You can't go home. Everything you knew outside those walls is suddenly gone.

Meanwhile, Walter seems to know considerably more about what's happening than anybody else.

So naturally, you're going to have a few questions for Walter.

And you're probably going to be a little irritated that he didn't bring this up before everybody packed their bags.

What's so effective about the premise is how quickly the questions start piling up.

Why these people? Why this house? Why Walter?

And probably the most important question: what exactly is Walter?

**SHOW:** Lake #1 first-look interiors, house diagram pages, Walter's emails, Lake #3 preview art.

### 04-the-people
**VO**

One of my favorite things about the concept is how the characters are assembled.

Each guest has a role.

There's an Artist, a Writer, a Comedian, an Accountant, a Scientist, a Reporter, an Acupuncturist, a Consultant, a Doctor, and a Pianist.

It's an odd assortment of people.

And that's immediately interesting to me, because these aren't necessarily the people you'd expect to find together in a typical survival story.

They've got different personalities, different backgrounds, different relationships with Walter, and different ways of responding to what's happening around them.

And Walter knows them.

That's what makes the whole thing feel so deliberately constructed.

You've got this strange little collection of people, living inside what almost feels like an experiment, trying to understand the rules of a situation nobody really volunteered for.

And as a reader, you're doing the same thing.

You're trying to understand why each person matters.

You start developing theories.

You start paying attention to conversations that might have seemed completely ordinary a few pages earlier.

I love that kind of storytelling. Give me a mystery that rewards paying attention, and you've pretty much got me.

**SHOW:** Sea cast lineup as a brief visual comparison, Lake #1 and #3 interiors, Lake main covers showing the individual guest symbols.

### 05-the-covers
**VO**

And this is where the comics themselves start doing something pretty clever.

Take a look at the covers.

Under the title, there's this little row of ten symbols.

You might initially assume they're decorative. Some kind of weird design element.

But each symbol corresponds to one of the guests.

And each issue highlights a different one.

Issue three highlights the third symbol. Issue seven highlights the seventh.

The story is organized around these characters, and the covers are quietly telling you whose story you're about to get.

There's even an additional symbol that appears in the row on Book Six.

I really appreciate when creators do this kind of thing.

Because it gives you another reason to go back and look at the individual issues.

And when you're reading a collected edition, like I was, those details help make the structure of the story more interesting.

There's something satisfying about realizing the book has been giving you information the entire time.

You just didn't necessarily know what you were looking at.

**SHOW:** Contact-main.jpg, Lake #1, #3, #6 and #7 main covers. Push in on the symbol row, then arrange all twelve covers chronologically.

### 06-the-creative-team
**VO**

The book was written by James Tynion the Fourth, with artwork by Álvaro Martínez Bueno, colors by Jordie Bellaire, and lettering by AndWorld Design.

It came out through DC's Black Label imprint, which is where DC publishes mature-reader material.

The first issue arrived in June of 2021, and the original series ran for twelve issues.

I think the artwork is a big part of why the premise works.

You've got this beautiful setting, all these people who could reasonably be enjoying a really nice vacation, and this growing sense that something is profoundly wrong.

It's a great contrast.

The setting almost feels too comfortable for the story that's happening inside it.

And the character-driven structure gives the creative team plenty of opportunities to make ordinary interactions feel uncomfortable.

A conversation in a beautiful house can become pretty unsettling when nobody involved understands the situation they're in.

Apparently, you don't need a haunted basement when you've got Walter handling the guest list.

And Tynion has talked pretty openly about how personal this story is to him.

Which brings us to something I found particularly interesting about Walter.

**SHOW:** Lake #1 cover, Lake #1 and #8 preview interiors, house diagram pages, Walter character artwork from sourced preview interiors.

### 07-the-walter-problem
**ON CAMERA | Bookshelf transition**

Walter might be the most interesting part of the whole thing for me.

And when you hear what James Tynion has said about him, it gets a little stranger.

**SHOW:** Tony at bookshelf. Cut to Walter art from Lake interiors.

**VO**

At C2E2 in 2026, Tynion talked about how deeply personal *The Nice House on the Lake* was.

And he said something I found fascinating.

He described Walter as a villain based on himself.

He even said Walter looks like him on purpose.

Now, obviously, I'm not suggesting James Tynion is secretly inviting people to suspicious vacation properties.

Although if he does invite you somewhere, maybe ask a few follow-up questions.

But that detail changes how I think about the character.

Because Walter is such a strange combination of things.

He's familiar to these people. He's the guy who brought them together.

He also happens to be hiding something rather significant.

And there's this uncomfortable relationship between the person organizing everything and the people whose lives are being organized.

You can interpret quite a bit through that relationship.

Especially knowing the writer deliberately put something of himself into Walter.

**SHOW:** Walter interiors from Lake #1 and #6, Walter's emails, relevant Lake main cover.

### 08-the-success
**VO**

And this wasn't some obscure little book that disappeared after twelve issues.

*The Nice House on the Lake* won the Eisner Award for Best New Series in 2022.

Tynion also won Best Writer that year.

In 2023, Tynion won Best Writer again, and Jordie Bellaire won Best Coloring, with *Nice House* among their credited work.

The series also picked up a major award at the Angoulême International Comics Festival in France in 2024.

So clearly, quite a few people thought this was worth reading.

The first issue even went back for a second printing, and there's an enormous assortment of variant covers for that first book.

ComicVine lists twenty-one variants and retailer exclusives for issue one alone.

As somebody interested in the collecting side of comics, I think that's fun to look through.

You've got all these different artistic interpretations of the same story, some with very different approaches to the cover.

And yet, the regular covers have their own little visual language running through the entire series.

I think that makes them especially satisfying to see together.

**SHOW:** Contact-variants.jpg, Lake #1 second-print cover, Lake trade covers, Deluxe Edition cover, contact-main.jpg.

### 09-back-to-the-house
**VO**

One thing worth mentioning is that I read *The Nice House on the Lake* in collected editions.

And for a story like this, I think that's a pretty enjoyable way to experience it.

You've got twelve issues to work through, with each chapter giving you another character to understand and another piece of the puzzle.

The original series had a couple of publication breaks along the way.

There was a gap between issues six and seven, and another between nine and ten.

So readers following it monthly had to spend a little more time wondering what was going to happen next.

I can certainly appreciate the appeal of that.

But personally, I tend to enjoy sitting down with a trade paperback and getting a good chunk of the story at once.

Especially with mysteries.

I want to remember the little details from the previous chapter without having to go dig through a stack of comics to remind myself who said what.

And this book gives you plenty of things worth remembering.

**SHOW:** Lake Vol. 1 and Vol. 2 covers, Lake #6 and #7 covers side by side, Lake #9 and #10 covers side by side, Lake preview pages.

### 10-spoiler-territory
**VO | SPOILER WARNING ON SCREEN**

I'm going to touch on a couple of developments from the middle of the first series here.

Nothing about the ending. And nothing that explains the bigger mystery.

But if you want to go in completely cold, this is your chance to skip ahead.

One of the interesting things about the house is that it has its own rules.

People can't die or get hurt inside it.

And the idea of being able to have essentially anything you want starts getting pretty uncomfortable when you realize how little control these people have over the situation.

Then there's Reg.

He's an eleventh guest who wasn't supposed to be there.

And that's a pretty significant complication when you consider how carefully everything else seems to have been arranged.

There's also the matter of Walter resetting everybody's memories partway through the story.

Which is a fairly rude thing to do to your friends.

Especially considering the circumstances.

All these developments add new questions about Walter, the house, and how much of what's happening has been deliberately planned.

And once you realize just how much control Walter has, it's hard to look at any of it quite the same way.

### 11-the-house-by-the-sea
**VO | SPOILER WARNING CLEARED**

Now, there's a reason I wanted to revisit this series.

In July of 2024, Tynion and the same creative team launched a follow-up called *The Nice House by the Sea*.

And as of October 2026, all twelve issues have been published.

That means the full twenty-four-issue saga is now available to read.

The second series brings us to a different house.

This one's by the sea, naturally.

And there's a new host named Max.

This time, instead of a group of friends, we've got a group of strangers who have been brought together with the promise that they'll survive and help shape humanity's future.

You've got another collection of people assigned different roles.

And according to Tynion, the relationships are considerably more antagonistic this time around.

These people don't particularly like one another.

Which sounds like a wonderful way to spend the apocalypse.

I haven't made it through *By the Sea* yet.

In fact, I want to go back through *Lake* and refresh my memory before diving in.

But the idea of taking that original premise and exploring it through a completely different set of relationships is exactly the sort of thing that gets my attention.

And now that the entire run is finished, I can work through it at my own pace.

### 12-the-recommendation
**ON CAMERA | Bookshelf ending**

So if you're somebody who enjoys a mystery, especially one where the characters are just as interesting as the circumstances they're trapped in, I'd recommend giving *The Nice House on the Lake* a shot.

You don't have to be a big horror-comics reader.

I'm certainly not.

And that's part of what made this one stand out to me.

The premise is fascinating, Walter's a genuinely interesting character, and there's a lot to pay attention to as the story unfolds.

I'm looking forward to revisiting it before getting into *By the Sea*.
