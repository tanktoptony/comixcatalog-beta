## short-1 · The secret on every cover (~40 s)

Every cover of The Nice House on the Lake has the same little row of ten symbols under the title.

They look like decoration. They're not.

Each one stands for one of the ten guests Walter invited to his lake house. And each issue lights up a different one.

Issue three lights the third. Issue seven lights the seventh. So the cover tells you whose story you're about to read before you even open it.

Book Six even adds a symbol that wasn't there before.

Twelve issues. Twelve lights. Go count them.

> Visuals: row zooms on #1, #3, #7, then #6's new hexagon, then all twelve covers.

## short-2 · Your weird friend invites you to a lake house (~40 s)

Your slightly weird friend Walter invites you and a bunch of your friends to spend a week at a gorgeous lake house.

Everything's arranged. You can have pretty much whatever you want.

Then the world outside ends.

You can't leave. You can't go home. And Walter seems to know a lot more about it than anybody else.

That's The Nice House on the Lake. A twelve-issue horror mystery from DC's Black Label, by James Tynion the Fourth and Álvaro Martínez Bueno. It won the Eisner for Best New Series.

And the sequel finished in October, so the whole story is out now.

> Visuals: #1 title spread, Walter's email, meteors (#5 cover), #1 cover, Vol. 2 cover's Eisner line, Sea #12.

## short-3 · The villain is the writer (~35 s)

In The Nice House on the Lake, a guy named Walter invites his friends to a lake house right before the world ends.

He knows more than he's telling. He's the villain.

And at C2E2 this year, writer James Tynion the Fourth said Walter is based on himself. He even said Walter looks a lot like him. On purpose.

He called it a very personal horror story.

So if a comic writer ever invites you somewhere for the weekend, maybe ask a couple of follow-up questions.
