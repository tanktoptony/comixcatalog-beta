### 02-back-in-west-lafayette

In October of two thousand three, I was a sophomore at Purdue University. Coheed and Cambria had just released their second album, *In Keeping Secrets of Silent Earth: 3*. I'm pretty sure I downloaded it first. I was buying CDs back then, too, so I'm sure I bought a copy. What I remember most clearly, though, is listening to it on my way to class.

**SHOW:** IKSSE:3 album cover, followed by a licensed band photo from the photos folder.

I was living in an off-campus apartment in West Lafayette, Indiana. I'd walk to class as the sun came up, listening to those opening notes of the title track. I can still picture it. The morning light, the walk, that enormous guitar sound. There are albums I remember liking. Then there are albums that bring an entire period of my life back with them. This is one of those.

**SHOW:** IKSSE:3 album cover, slow pan across artwork.

**CLIP:** "In Keeping Secrets of Silent Earth: 3" · Opening guitar passage, maximum 5 seconds.

We covered *The Second Stage Turbine Blade* in Part 2. That album was my introduction to the band, and it'll always have a place in my heart. But I might actually like this one more. This was the record that really cemented my following of Coheed. I was completely invested by this point, and I've stayed that way. I've seen them a couple dozen times since.

**SHOW:** SSTB album cover transitioning into IKSSE:3 album cover. Licensed band photograph.

And since we spent an entire episode introducing Coheed, Cambria, their children, and the rather unfortunate situation they found themselves in, we should probably discuss what happened next. Because I deliberately left you hanging last time.

### 03-the-family-and-the-ghost

The story picks up ten years after *The Second Stage Turbine Blade*. Claudio Kilgannon emerges from Shylos Ten, a prison planet used by the Red Army for interrogations and imprisonment. His family is gone. And the opening of this chapter finally confronts the tragedy I left unresolved in Part 2. In the comic, we learn that Coheed killed Josephine and the twins. It's a terrible beginning for a story about a young man trying to understand what's happened to everyone he loved.

**SHOW:** Official Coheed, Cambria, and Claudio character art. IKSSE:3 issue #1 cover.

And Claudio isn't exactly getting a clean start. He's haunted by the ghost of his father, whom only he can see and hear. They argue. Which is a particularly awful arrangement when you've already got a murdered family and an entire military force interested in you. Claudio has powers he hasn't used in ten years, and Wilhelm Ryan and General Mayo Deftinwolf know he's alive.

In the first issue, Claudio uses those powers again. A little girl calls him an angel. His response is that there aren't any angels anymore. That's a pretty effective way to establish where this character is emotionally. He's survived, but I don't think anybody would describe him as doing especially well.

### 04-hello-apollo

Now here's one of my favorite connections between the music and the comic. The album begins with a short track called "The Ring in Return." And the first words we hear are, "Hello Apollo, where should I begin?" That isn't just a strange little introduction Claudio Sanchez threw onto the record. The comic opens the same way, with Claudio telling the story to Apollo outside Newo Ikkin's house.

**SHOW:** IKSSE:3 album cover beside issue #1 cover, followed by Apollo and Newo character art.

**CLIP:** "The Ring in Return" · Opening spoken line, maximum 5 seconds.

I love that. You can read the comic and recognize the moment from the album. You can go back to the album and understand why those words are there. It's a direct connection, right at the beginning, and it rewards the kind of person who spends far too much time paying attention to album details. So, apparently, me.

**SHOW:** Apollo character art and issue #1 cover.

The title track follows, and it's over eight minutes long. It was already a huge part of my college soundtrack. Now, when I revisit it, I can place it beside this opening chapter of Claudio's story. The album gives the whole thing its atmosphere. The comic gives us characters, locations, and a much clearer idea of what we're dealing with.

**SHOW:** Album cover, Claudio art, Heaven's Fence map.

**CLIP:** "In Keeping Secrets of Silent Earth: 3" · Maximum 5 seconds.

### 05-the-war-outside

The story is bigger than Claudio's family. Wilhelm Ryan is still the Supreme Tri-Mage, Mayo Deftinwolf is still involved, and the Red Army is still a problem. Meanwhile, Jesse, also known as Inferno, is fighting his own war from a ship called the Grail Arbor. If you remember the K-B-I from Part 2, Jesse should already be familiar. ### 06-the-crowing

"The Crowing" might have been my favorite song when I first got the album. It's still one of the songs I most associate with this period of the band. There's something about the way these long, sprawling compositions work. You're several minutes in, the song keeps developing, and somehow you end up feeling like you've traveled somewhere by the time it's finished.

**SHOW:** Album cover and Claudio character art.

**CLIP:** "The Crowing" · Maximum 5 seconds.

The important story connection is right there in the title. The Crowing is Claudio's role in this story. He's discovering powers he hasn't used for a decade. Other people have already taken an interest in what he might be capable of. Ryan and Mayo want him stopped. So while Claudio's still trying to understand his own life, there's a much larger conflict forming around him.

**SHOW:** Claudio, Ryan, and Mayo character art. Issue #4 cover.

I think that's part of what makes Claudio interesting to follow. At this point, he's somebody who has lost almost everything and has very little reason to trust the people around him. And now he's supposed to figure out what to do with these powers. That's a lot to ask of anybody, particularly someone whose father is following him around as a ghost.

### 07-blood-red-summer

Then we get "Blood Red Summer." This and "A Favor House Atlantic" became the songs that a lot of people outside the existing Coheed audience encountered. "A Favor House Atlantic" was released as a single in June two thousand four, and "Blood Red Summer" followed in November. I don't remember diving immediately into the singles when I first got the album. I was listening to the whole record.

**SHOW:** Album cover, licensed early band photograph, Claudio art.

And that's something I appreciate about revisiting this music through the comics. Songs I originally knew by melody, or by whatever lyrics I could make out, now sit alongside an actual published story. Of course, it also means I occasionally discover that whatever I thought a song was about twenty years ago was probably miles off. That's part of the fun.

### 08-ambellina-and-sizer

Let's get back to the comic, because Claudio isn't making this journey alone. We're introduced to Ambellina, a Prise who's been cast out by her own kind and forced to guide him. There's also Sizer, a disassembled I-Robot. Together, they're looking for Inferno, hoping to get some answers. Which sounds like a reasonable plan until you remember this is *The Amory Wars*, where reasonable plans have a pretty short life expectancy.

**SHOW:** Ambellina, Sizer, Claudio, and Inferno character art.

Inferno has his own operation underway. His laboratory is building two I-Robots named Chase and Sizer. Chase has the mind of a grown woman in the body of a little girl. That's quite a bit of information to absorb, and it's part of why I appreciate having the comics available. There are characters and relationships here that deserve more attention than I could give them just listening to the album on the way to class.

**SHOW:** Chase, Sizer, and Inferno character art.

### 09-the-camper-velourium

Now we reach three consecutive songs that share a title: *The Camper Velourium*. "Faint of Hearts," "Backend of Forever," and "Al the Killer." In the story, the Camper Velourium is a ship. Al the Killer is a freighter pilot. Claudio and his companions encounter trouble aboard that ship, so the titles give us another direct connection between the album and the comics.

**SHOW:** Issues #1–4 covers, Heaven's Fence map.

**CLIP:** "The Camper Velourium I: Faint of Hearts" · Maximum 5 seconds.

I especially want to talk about "Backend of Forever." This is almost my favorite Coheed song, period. And that's saying something after following the band for more than twenty years. It's one of those songs I can revisit endlessly. I love having this whole three-song stretch sitting together on the record. Even before you dig into the story, there's something satisfying about following a sequence of songs that clearly belong together.

We've got a named freighter pilot, a named ship, and an entire storyline associated with these titles. There's a lot more to examine than I realized when I was first listening to these records.

### 12-the-hidden-song

But the album still has one more trick. If you owned the CD, you might remember finishing "The Light & the Glass" and discovering a bunch of tracks that seemed to contain absolutely nothing. Eleven silent tracks, in fact. They're called "A Lot of Nothing I" through "A Lot of Nothing XI." And then, on track twenty-three, there's another song waiting for you: "21:13."

**SHOW:** Album cover.

**[SCAN: Tony's CD, disc, packaging, and track listing.]**

I loved that sort of thing. I still do. There's something appealing about feeling like you've been let in on a secret. You had to be paying attention, or at least curious enough to keep going. "21:13" also brings back musical ideas from "Time Consumer" and "I-Robot" on the first album. So even this little hidden discovery connects us to where the series began.

**SHOW:** SSTB cover dissolving into IKSSE:3 cover. Keywork diagram.

**CLIP:** "21:13" · Maximum 5 seconds.

Some digital versions and reissues remove the silent tracks and "21:13" altogether. That's a shame, because the strange way you found that song was part of the experience for me. The vinyl includes it, and I've got the album on both formats. I'll take a little unnecessary mystery in my record collection any day.

### 13-from-album-to-comic

The album came out in October two thousand three. The twelve-issue comic series arrived years later, beginning in May two thousand ten and finishing in June two thousand eleven. It was published by BOOM! Studios with Evil Ink, written by Claudio Sanchez and Peter David. Chris Burnham handled early artwork, with other artists including Kyle Strahm and Aaron Kuder contributing over the run.

**SHOW:** Issues #1, #6, #8, and #12 covers.

I was buying the individual issues as they came out. I've still got the whole run. And I remember thinking the books felt a little low-rent compared with some of the major comics I was following at the time. That's probably not the most glowing endorsement, but it's what I remember. I was already invested in the band and the story, so naturally I wanted these books on my shelf.

**SHOW:** Issues #1–12 cover montage.

**[SCAN: Tony's complete original issue run spread across a table.]**

There are some interesting collector details here, too. Issue one had a convention-exclusive cover limited to five hundred copies. Issue five had a New York Comic Con cover. And issue nine had a C2E2 exclusive limited to three hundred copies. As a Chicago collector, that last one especially gets my attention. I don't own either of the numbered exclusives, unfortunately. I wish I did.

**SHOW:** Actual #1 convention-exclusive, #5 NYCC, and #9 C2E2 variant covers.

The series was also collected into three trade paperbacks, four issues each, and eventually hardcover editions and a Complete Collection. The Ultimate Edition is another route into the story. It's interesting to me how many different physical ways there are to collect what began as an album I listened to walking to class.

**SHOW:** 2012 Ultimate Edition cover, issues #1 and #12, editions-tab screenshot.

### 14-neverender-and-the-legacy

One thing Coheed has done over the years is give these albums another life onstage. In two thousand eight, the original Neverender concerts featured four albums performed in full, one each night. Chicago's shows were at the Riviera Theatre, with *In Keeping Secrets* on night two. In two thousand fourteen, they toured this entire album again.

**SHOW:** Licensed live photographs, album cover.

I didn't make it to either of those particular runs. My little brother and I did attend the tenth-anniversary show for *The Second Stage Turbine Blade*, though, and I've caught Coheed plenty of other times. I think that's part of why these records have stuck with me. They're tied to different stages of my life. I've been following this band since I was a college kid.

**SHOW:** SSTB and IKSSE:3 album covers side by side, licensed live photograph.

And the story has kept finding new ways to reach people. There was even a twentieth-anniversary S.S. Neverender cruise with the *Raiders of Silent Earth: 3* theme. The album also earned Gold certification, and its original release peaked at number fifty-two on the Billboard two hundred. Quite a journey for a story that once came to me on a burned CD.
